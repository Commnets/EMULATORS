Living document based on verifiable capabilities. It must be updated with its Spanish twin whenever C264, TED, memory, files, peripherals, or shared tools change.

1. CORE IDEA

One emulator recreates the character of the C16, C116, and Plus/4 around their central component, TED, while EMULATORS tools expose the relationship among CPU, video, sound, timers, and memory.

2. REFERENCE-LEVEL CAPABILITIES

- Explicit C16, C116, or Commodore Plus/4 selection, in PAL or NTSC.
- 7501 CPU with slow/fast operation governed by TED activity.
- C16/C116 with 16, 32, or 64 KiB; Plus/4 with 64 KiB and integrated 3-Plus-1 ROM.
- ROM/RAM switching and KERNAL, BASIC, 3-Plus-1, and cartridge banks; hardware distinctions between C16/C116 and Plus/4, including ACIA and the second 6529B where applicable.
- 7360/8360 TED with PAL/NTSC raster, raster IRQ, three timers, standard, multicolor, and extended-color text, high-resolution and multicolor bitmap, border, scrolling, and the family's own palette/luminance model.
- Two-voice TED sound, with register-controlled tone and noise.
- Keyboard matrix, two joysticks, 1531 cassette, and serial bus.
- English, French, Swedish, and Hungarian ROM/interface variants.
- CRT, TAP, PRG, D64, keystroke, and raw files; pulse-level cassette or fast injection.
- Cartridges, up to four logical 1541 drives, and two serial printers with text or PostScript output.
- Shared diagnostic console plus TED-specific status, screen, attribute, bitmap, character, and IRQ/raster visualization commands.

3. WHAT MAKES IT UNIQUE

The C264 series is often reduced to “another Commodore.” Here it is an explicit family: machine differences remain visible and TED is exposed as an integrated system, including CPU speed changes and all three timers. Comparing C16/C116/Plus/4 through one console makes this especially valuable for preservation and analysis.

4. BENEFITS

- Retro: one gateway to C16, C116, and Plus/4 software on tape, disk, PRG, and cartridge.
- Development: visible TED, CPU, and memory state with reproducible PAL/NTSC and 16/32/64 KiB scenarios.
- Education: a clear demonstration of one integrated chip arbitrating video, sound, timing, and memory access.
- Framework: the debugger and peripherals are shared with C64/VIC-20 while C264's architecture remains distinct.

5. READY-TO-USE MESSAGES

Short line: “C16, C116, and Plus/4 in one observable TED lab, with PAL/NTSC models and configurable memory.”

Pitch: “EMULATORS does not treat C264 as a superficial variant: it models its machines, memory maps, and I/O distinctions, and exposes the TED coordinating video, sound, timers, and CPU speed.”

6. HONEST LIMITS

- Do not claim complete compatibility or cycle accuracy without published results.
- 1541 units are trap-based logical D64 simulations, not complete electronics, firmware, GCR, and mechanics emulations.
- Printers generate logical text/PostScript output; they do not reproduce all physical hardware.
- Validate cartridges and highly timing-sensitive software case by case.
- C16 and C116 currently share the same functional class; selection does not imply a physically distinct keyboard or case recreation.

7. POSITIONING

“A broad, transparent C264-family implementation, comparable with reference emulators in essential functions and differentiated by its instrumentation and reusable architecture.” Avoid “most compatible” until a comparative matrix exists.

8. TRACEABILITY

Review include/src/C264; include/src/COMMODORE/TED, C6529B, and ACIA; COMMODORE cassette, serial, D64, and printing components; setups/C264; and the CORE/EMULATORS console.

9. MAINTENANCE

Every new claim requires active code and, for timing, format, or peripheral behavior, a reproducible test. Record the affected machine, region, RAM, and I/O path.
