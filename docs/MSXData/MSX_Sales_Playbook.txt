1. CORE IDEA

EMULATORS' MSX is not a generic machine with a swapped ROM: it is a model-driven architecture in which Z80, memory slots, VDP, PSG, PPI, keyboard, and cassette can be inspected as a complete system.

2. REFERENCE-LEVEL CAPABILITIES

- 3.58 MHz MSX1 core with Standard, Spectravideo SVI-728, Sony HB-10P, Philips VG-8010, Canon V-20, and SVI-738 models in the current implementation.
- Model-selectable PAL and NTSC variants.
- Model-specific RAM: 16 KiB in SVI-728, HB-10P, and SVI-738; 32 KiB in VG-8010; 64 KiB in Canon V-20.
- MSX memory map with four 16 KiB pages, four primary slots, expanded subslots where the model defines them, primary selection through PPI, and the inverted subslot register at FFFFh.
- TMS9918A-family VDP with 16 KiB VRAM, 98h/99h ports, Text 40, Graphics I, Graphics II, and Multicolor modes.
- Up to 32 sprites, four-per-line limit, size/magnification, collision, and overflow status.
- AY-3-8910 PSG with three tone channels, shared noise and envelope, plus ports for joystick, cassette, and model signals.
- 8255 PPI for slot selection, keyboard matrix, and system functions; standard VDP, PSG, and PPI ports.
- Model ROM/keyboard variants: English and Spanish (SVI-728), English (Sony/Canon), English and French (Philips), and English, German, and Swedish (SVI-738).
- Block-based CAS reading and writing plus automated keyboard input.
- Shared console plus VDP/PSG status, name/pattern, sprite, editable VRAM, and VDP event commands.

3. WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT

MSX was a standard spanning diverse machines. EMULATORS reflects that reality: the model selects ROM, language, RAM, video, sound, keyboard, and slot layout. Memory architecture is not flattened; pages, slots, and subslots remain visible and actionable. That combination of diversity and observability is the differentiator.

4. BENEFITS

- Retro: several manufacturers and regions inside one coherent experience.
- Development: joint inspection of Z80, VDP, PSG, PPI, ports, VRAM, and slots.
- Education: a direct example of one common standard supporting different vendor implementations.
- Preservation: explicit per-model configuration and regional ROMs rather than hidden differences behind one profile.

5. READY-TO-USE MESSAGES

Short line: “MSX from the inside: multiple models, real slots, TMS9918A, AY-3-8910, and fully observable PPI.”

Pitch: “EMULATORS models MSX as a platform, not a single ROM. Each machine contributes its own memory, language, and configuration to a verifiable Z80, VDP, PSG, PPI, and slot core; the console exposes everything from a sprite or sound channel to the slot serving each page.”

6. HONEST LIMITS

- The implemented graphics/system core is MSX1/TMS9918A. Do not promise V9938/V9958, MSX2 modes, memory mapper, disk, or cartridges until implemented and validated.
- Although historical UI text labels SVI-738 as MSX2, its current class inherits the MSX1 model and uses that core; sales material must keep it within implemented MSX1 scope until the architecture is corrected or extended.
- Currently supported user formats are CAS and keystroke sequences; do not present ROM/cartridge or disk-image workflows as available.
- Do not claim total compatibility or cycle accuracy without a test matrix.
- Validate keyboard, joystick, cassette, slot, and ROM details per model before citing specific software.

7. POSITIONING

“A transparent, multi-model MSX1 platform with observable slot architecture and subsystems.” Its commercial strength is understanding and comparing implementations, not claiming every MSX generation.

8. TRACEABILITY

Review include/src/MSX: Model, Memory, PPI8255, PortManager, VDP/TMS9918A, PSG/AY38910, OSIO, FileIO, and Datasette; setups/MSX; Z80 CPU; and CORE/EMULATORS console.

9. MAINTENANCE

Every claim must identify model, effective generation, region, RAM, and chip. Slot, port, VDP, sound, or CAS changes require reproducible per-model tests.
